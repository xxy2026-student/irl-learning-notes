Latent-Dynamics-Conditioned Flow Policy

From Marginal Multimodality to Environment-Conditioned Imitation

Learning which mode is valid under hidden environment dynamics

Flow Matching can learn multiple expert behaviors — but how does the policy know which behavior is correct in the current hidden environment?

Standard imitation \( \pi(a \mid s) \)
Our goal \( \pi(a \mid s, z) \)

The challenge is not representing multiple modes — it is selecting the mode compatible with the current latent dynamics.

01Problem setup

Expert data is generated by hidden environments

Training data. Expert trajectories collected from different hidden environments — observed: \( \tau_i \), unobserved: \( z_i \).
Key structure. Every trajectory is generated under one fixed but unknown environment parameter \( z_i \).
wind condition friction / mass system dynamics

02What standard flow matching learns

Standard Flow Matching learns the marginal behavior distribution

The expert’s true policy depends on the environment, \( \pi_E(a \mid s, z) \). Trained on pooled trajectories with \( z \) ignored, Flow Matching fits the marginal:

\[ \pi_{\mathrm{marg}}(a \mid s) \;=\; \int \pi_E(a \mid s, z)\, p(z \mid s)\, dz . \]

Flow Matching correctly learns the multimodal distribution — but loses the environment-to-mode correspondence.

The model is not failing to learn the data distribution.
It is learning the wrong object for deployment.

03Why deployment fails

At test time there is only one environment — the marginal does not know which

Training

many latent environments \( z_1, z_2, \ldots \) → many expert modes. The standard flow policy learns \( \pi(a \mid s) \), containing all modes:

faithful to the pooled data — the training objective is satisfied

Deployment \( z = z^\star \)

the real environment is fixed — say \( z^\star = z_1 \). The correct policy would be \( \pi_E(a \mid s, z^\star) \), but the standard flow policy still transports its samples to every mode of \( \pi(a \mid s) \):

Mode 1 — matches \( z^\star \) ✓ Mode 2 — other environment ✗

multimodal imitation ≠ environment-adaptive imitation

04Goal — the target object

From \( p(x) \) to \( p(x \mid z) \)

We do not only want to model multimodality — we want to recover the latent condition that explains it.

Current generative modeling

\[ p(x) = \int p(x \mid z)\, p(z)\, dz \]

the mixture is baked into one distribution

Desired conditional modeling

\[ p(x \mid z) \]

environment-resolved

\[ \pi(a \mid s) \;\longrightarrow\; \pi(a \mid s, z) \]

in the policy setting: condition action generation on the latent dynamics

05Key challenge — \( z \) is also unknown at deployment

The condition itself must be inferred from interaction

Even with a trained conditional policy \( \pi_\theta(a \mid s, z) \), the true \( z^\star \) is never observed. It must be inferred from the interaction history:

\[ h_t = (s_0, a_0, s_1, \ldots, s_t), \qquad z \sim q_\phi(z \mid h_t), \]
\[ \pi(a_t \mid s_t, h_t) \;=\; \int \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t, z)\, q_\phi(z \mid h_t)\, dz . \]

06Proposal — conceptual architecture

Two modules: identify the dynamics, then condition the flow

Module A

Latent dynamics identification

Input \( (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) \)  or  history \( h_t \)
Output \( \hat z_t \)  or  \( q_\phi(z \mid h_t) \)

\( z \) should explain transition dynamics, not merely action modes.

\[ P_\psi(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, z) \]

latent-conditioned dynamics model used to shape \( z \)

Module B

Conditional flow matching policy

Input \( s_t,\; z,\; x_\lambda,\; \lambda \)
Field \( v_\theta(x_\lambda, \lambda;\, s_t, z) \)
Output action / action chunk \( a_t \)
\[ h_t \;\rightarrow\; q_\phi(z \mid h_t) \;\rightarrow\; \hat z_t \;\rightarrow\; \text{conditional flow } v_\theta \;\rightarrow\; a_t, \qquad (s_t, a_t) \;\xrightarrow{\;P_{z^\star}\;} s_{t+1} \;\rightarrow\; h_{t+1} \]

conceptual components — not committed network architectures

\( \lambda \in [0,1] \): flow time;  \( x_\lambda \): intermediate sample along the probability path.

07Why infer \( z \) from dynamics?

A latent learned from actions alone may be the wrong latent

✗ action clustering

behavior latent

fitting \( q(z \mid \tau) \) to action patterns can collapse to “\( z=1 \): go left, \( z=2 \): go right”

✓ transition prediction

environment latent

require \( z \) to explain how the world responds: \( \,s_{t+1} \sim P_z(\cdot \mid s_t, a_t) \)

Environment latents should be identified by how the world responds to actions — not by how the expert happens to act.

08Illustration — minimal toy example

Point mass under hidden wind

\( s_t = (x_t, y_t) \) \( a_t = (u_t, v_t) \) \( z \in \{-c, +c\} \)  (horizontal wind)
\[ x_{t+1} = x_t + u_t + z, \qquad y_{t+1} = y_t + v_t . \]

The goal is straight ahead. Under wind \( z=-c \) the expert must push right; under wind \( z=+c \) it must push left — so near the same state, the expert action distribution is bimodal.

true wind
Standard FM samples \( \pi(a \mid s) \)
\( q(z=-c) \) —
\( q(z=+c) \) —

\( z \) is never inferred — every step samples either mode

Ours \( q_\phi(z \mid h_t) \)  +  \( \pi_\theta(a \mid s, z) \)
\( q(z=-c \mid h_t) \) 0.50
\( q(z=+c \mid h_t) \) 0.50

the first observed transition reveals \( z \) — then only the compatible mode is used

09Research question — theory

When is the latent environment identifiable?

For two environments \( z \neq z' \): if some visited state–action pair separates their dynamics,

\[ P_z(\cdot \mid s, a) \;\neq\; P_{z'}(\cdot \mid s, a), \]

then interaction carries information that distinguishes them. A candidate distinguishability quantity:

\[ \mathcal{I}(z, z') \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{(s,a) \sim d}\!\left[\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left( P_z(\cdot \mid s, a) \,\middle\|\, P_{z'}(\cdot \mid s, a) \right) \right] . \]
\( \mathcal{I}(z, z') = 0 \)
impossible to distinguish from interaction
\( \mathcal{I}(z, z') > 0 \)
latent identification may be possible
dynamics distinguishability latent identification correct mode selection deployment performance

target theoretical route — every link is an open question, none is established

\( d \): state–action visitation distribution under the deployed policy.

10Hypothesis — target error decomposition

Two errors, only one of which flow matching addresses

\[ J(\pi^\star_{z^\star}) - J(\hat\pi) \;\lesssim\; C_1\, \epsilon_{\mathrm{flow}} \;+\; C_2\, \epsilon_{\mathrm{latent}} . \]
\( \epsilon_{\mathrm{flow}} \) error in learning the conditional policy \( \pi(a \mid s, z) \)
\( \epsilon_{\mathrm{latent}} \) error in identifying the current environment

Standard marginal Flow Matching mainly targets the first term — and ignores the second.

Stated as a working hypothesis for the shape of a bound (\( C_1, C_2 \): unspecified problem-dependent constants) — nothing here is proven.
